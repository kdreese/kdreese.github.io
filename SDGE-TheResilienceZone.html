San Diego Gas & Electric: The Resilience Zone

Project Overview

The Resilience Zone at San Diego Gas & Electric is an educational play space that I worked on at Trivium Interactive. The space consists of nine games all dealing with different themes and challenges of renewable energy and power grid management. The games are all networked into an overarching gameplay system manager that syncs the gameplay and rounds across all players, tracks their scores from game to game, and is easily piloted by a docent in the space. My main contributions to The Resilience Zone include heading the internal game design process and developing one of the games in the space: Lights Out!

Lights Out! is a game inspired by the gameplay of Battleship, where players place physical markers onto the board to control an emergency response vehicle. After each marker is placed, the players are given feedback through environmental clues as to how close they are to the emergency and what type of emergency it might be. Using these clues, the players continue to navigate closer to the emergency until they find it or run out of time or markers. At the end, they are quizzed on what the emergency was and are given points based on how close they were to finding the emergency, how long it took them if they did find it, and if they correctly identified information about the emergency at the end.

Team Responsibilities and Lessons Learned

  • Lead internal game design
  • Co-created game design document
  • Balanced scoring criteria across all nine games
  • Fully developed gameplay and UI programming of Lights Out!
  • Integrated gameplay with IR overlay hardware
  • Integrated gameplay with custom networked control system
  • Oversaw user testing and integrated user feedback
  • Real world logistics are just as important as in game logistics
    One of the major challenges in designing the games and overall system of The Resilience Zone was getting all of the games, representing disparate ideas and teaching points, to all roughly take the same amount of time. Additionally, so the experience could be experienced by multiple school groups in a day, we needed to make sure the full experience fit into a tight, 90 minute to 2 hour block. Fitting all of the content into a quick, entertaining package was challenging, but resulted in a project that has been a big hit with student groups throughout the San Diego area.
  • Test with as close to final conditions and hardware as possible
    Throughout production we had difficulty getting an accurate reading from the IR overlay that we had fixed to our testing monitor with clamps. While we knew the inexact attachment wasn't how the overlay was going to installed on site, the amount of time that was spent trying to improve the results, when the final installment ended up fixing all of the problems we were having, could have been spent better focusing on other parts of the game. Additionally, this was a very complicated system with nine games running in tandem. While each game had been tested well individually, there were many problems that needed to be solved on site due to insufficient testing of the full system. Better testing would have made many parts of this project smoother.
